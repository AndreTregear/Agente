Failure Analysis

Why Quechua Failed

  • Language misidentification: Model consistently identifies Quechua audio as Filipino, Malay, or Spanish — it has no Quechua phoneme category in its pretrained knowledge (52 languages, Quechua not among them)
  • Phonological hallucination: Without Quechua-specific phoneme recognition, the model maps Quechua sounds to the nearest phonemes from known languages, producing garbled output
  • Ejective consonants: Quechua p', t', k', q' (ejectives) have no counterpart in any of the model's 52 languages
  • LoRA insufficient: Rank 32 LoRA on the LM decoder may not be enough to teach entirely new phonemes — the encoder (AuT) is frozen and doesn't recognize Quechua sounds
  • Data quality unknown: Common Voice Quechua recordings vary in quality; some may have background noise or non-standard pronunciations

Why Spanish Works

  • Spanish is one of Qwen3-ASR's 52 pretrained languages
  • 20.2% WER on FLEURS Spanish test set is competitive with published baselines
  • The LoRA training didn't catastrophically forget Spanish — good sign for the training setup

Next Steps

  1. Unfreeze the AuT encoder — The core problem is that the frozen audio encoder doesn't recognize Quechua phonemes. LoRA on the encoder (not just decoder) is needed.
  2. Higher LoRA rank — Increase from 32 to 64 or 128. Research shows rank 64 outperforms 32 for new language acquisition.
  3. Bilingual training from the start — Mix Quechua + Spanish data in training (60/40 or 70/30) to leverage Spanish phoneme overlap.
  4. Codec simulation / data augmentation — Add noise, speed perturbation, pitch shifting to training data.
  5. Whisper-based evaluation — Compare against Whisper large-v3 zero-shot as a more meaningful baseline.
  6. System prompt engineering — Qwen3-ASR supports context biasing via system prompt. Test with "Transcribe this Quechua audio" prompt.
  7. Target SOTA — Current SOTA is 22.75% WER (wav2letter++ with data augmentation). We need to beat that.
